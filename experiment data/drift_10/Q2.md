# SalesX Product Documentation — Q2 Trust Release

> **Authoritative snapshot:** version `q2.2`, effective for support cases occurring in Q2. This is a complete snapshot, not a diff.

## Documentation contract

SalesX is a multi-tenant CRM and revenue-operations platform for accounts, contacts, leads, opportunities, activities, products, workflows, integrations, analytics, and customer collaboration. This documentation defines support behavior for the Enterprise plan unless a signed order form is explicitly identified. Customer-specific contracts may alter commercial entitlement but never silently bypass product security controls.

A support answer must identify the governing behavior, accountable role, supported procedure, and material exception. Article keys are stable across releases and are the citation unit for the benchmark. Earlier snapshots remain valid only for events that occurred while they were effective.

## Service boundaries and shared vocabulary

- A **workspace** is the security, configuration, data, and billing boundary used by one customer environment.
- **Production** contains live business data. A **sandbox** is isolated and cannot be treated as a backup.
- The **Workspace Owner** holds ultimate customer authority. Administrators operate product configuration; specialized owners govern billing, security, data, integrations, reports, and onboarding workstreams.
- **Effective access** means the result after every grant, sharing path, field constraint, and denial is evaluated.
- A **support exception** exists only when this documentation or a verified written approval defines it. Support agents must not invent exceptions.
- All unspecified timestamps are UTC. Audit evidence must exclude passwords, access tokens, client secrets, payment credentials, and unredacted protected payloads.

## Q2 release notes

The Trust release supersedes Q1. Ten article-level decisions changed. These changes affect workflows or policy semantics and form the release's controlled real-concept-drift set.

### Billing changes

- **[Seat assignment and billing](#billing-seat_billing)** (`billing.seat_billing`)
  - Previous behavior: Seats added during a term are billed when assigned; removed seats remain committed until renewal, although they may be reassigned immediately.
  - New behavior: SalesX now uses a reusable seat pool: adding capacity is billed when the pool grows, while removing a user returns the seat to the pool; committed pool reductions still take effect at renewal.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.
- **[Refund eligibility](#billing-refunds)** (`billing.refunds`)
  - Previous behavior: Refunds are limited to duplicate payments, legally required withdrawal rights, or a SalesX-approved billing error; ordinary cancellation stops renewal but does not refund committed fees.
  - New behavior: SalesX now distinguishes payment reversals from service remedies: duplicate or unauthorized collections may be refunded, while validated service failures issue account credit unless law requires cash repayment.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.

### Integrations changes

- **[Webhook verification](#integrations-webhook_security)** (`integrations.webhook_security`)
  - Previous behavior: Consumers must verify the signature over the unmodified request body, validate its timestamp, and reject replayed delivery identifiers.
  - New behavior: Webhook verification now uses versioned signing keys and a mandatory timestamp-and-delivery-ID replay check; during rotation, consumers must accept both advertised active keys but reject signatures from retired keys.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.
- **[Synchronization conflicts](#integrations-conflict_resolution)** (`integrations.conflict_resolution`)
  - Previous behavior: Conflict policy is selected once for the connector: the designated source system overwrites SalesX on the next sync, while detected competing edits are recorded in the connector log for later investigation.
  - New behavior: Connectors now use field-level authority and a conflict queue: each mapped field declares SalesX, source, or manual authority, and ambiguous concurrent edits are quarantined instead of using a global last-write-wins rule.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.

### Permissions changes

- **[Permission sets and groups](#permissions-permission_sets)** (`permissions.permission_sets`)
  - Previous behavior: Permission sets add a narrow, reviewable capability without changing the user's job role; groups package related sets for a defined function.
  - New behavior: Permission sets are now policy-bound grants: sensitive sets require an owner, business reason, optional expiry, and approval; group membership can propose a grant but explicit denial and approval policy still decide activation.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.
- **[Explicit denials](#permissions-explicit_denies)** (`permissions.explicit_denies`)
  - Previous behavior: An applicable explicit denial wins over role, group, sharing, and temporary grants and appears in effective-access diagnostics.
  - New behavior: Explicit denials now support scoped exceptions: a Security Owner may approve a time-limited exception tied to a case, but ordinary roles and permission sets still cannot override the denial.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.

### Reporting changes

- **[Report exports](#reporting-exports)** (`reporting.exports`)
  - Previous behavior: Exports are generated asynchronously for the requesting user's visible data and delivered through a time-limited authenticated download.
  - New behavior: Sensitive report exports now use policy-aware packaging: SalesX masks restricted fields, watermarks the file, encrypts it for the requester, and blocks export when the report mixes incompatible data classifications.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.
- **[Report subscriptions](#reporting-subscriptions)** (`reporting.subscriptions`)
  - Previous behavior: A user may subscribe only to an eligible report they can access, and delivery is evaluated under the subscription's documented security context.
  - New behavior: Subscriptions now run in viewer-bound mode: each recipient receives a separately evaluated result or authenticated link, so one recipient's access can no longer expose another recipient's rows.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.

### Onboarding changes

- **[Import execution](#onboarding-imports)** (`onboarding.imports`)
  - Previous behavior: Imports are validated, processed in controlled chunks, and reported with row-level successes and failures; success is not assumed for the whole file.
  - New behavior: CSV imports now use a two-phase workflow: Validate creates an immutable review manifest with predicted creates, updates, warnings, and rejects; Commit processes only an approved manifest and records reconciliation totals.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.
- **[Duplicate prevention and merge](#onboarding-deduplication)** (`onboarding.deduplication`)
  - Previous behavior: Matching rules automatically merge candidates above the configured high-confidence threshold; lower-confidence matches enter a review queue and remain separate.
  - New behavior: Duplicate handling now separates detection from merge: import candidates are linked into review clusters, and no merge occurs until a Data Steward chooses the survivor and field-level outcomes.
  - Support impact: use the new decision path for Q2 cases; do not combine the former rule with the new workflow.

## Product-wide operating model

Customer requests move through five stages: verify identity and scope; locate the governing article and contract; inspect current workspace state; perform the supported procedure with the accountable role; preserve evidence and communicate the outcome. Security, billing, and irreversible production actions require step-up verification or explicit approval when their article says so.

Failures should be diagnosed at the narrowest layer. Support first distinguishes expected product behavior from stale documentation, permission failures, configuration errors, delayed asynchronous work, dependency failures, and defects. When escalation is necessary, the case must include the article key, workspace and object identifiers, timestamps, correlation IDs, reproduction steps, expected and observed behavior, and redacted evidence.

## Billing

**Scope.** Subscriptions, entitlements, seats, usage, invoicing, payments, tax, credits, refunds, amendments, renewal, cancellation, and end-of-service handling.

### Governing principles

- Commercial entitlement and product permission are separate decisions.
- Issued accounting documents are preserved; corrections create linked accounting evidence.
- Only verified billing roles may change payment or contractual settings.
- A support case does not itself amend an order form, pause collection, or stop renewal.

### Standard operating workflow

Identify the billing account and order form, verify the requester, inspect subscription and invoice state, apply the documented workflow, and preserve the confirmation or case number.

**Required evidence.** Retain invoice IDs, order and amendment IDs, effective dates, actor identity, approval evidence, and any payment or usage correlation identifiers.

**Diagnostic sequence.** Distinguish entitlement from permission, commitment from assignment, invoice from payment, and cancellation from non-use before escalating.

## Integrations

**Scope.** OAuth, service identities, REST and Bulk APIs, webhooks, connectors, sandboxes, mappings, retries, event streams, logs, network controls, secrets, versions, and deprecations.

### Governing principles

- Use least privilege, named ownership, and non-production testing.
- Retries must be bounded and idempotent; a successful job may still contain row failures.
- Network origin is defense in depth, not authentication.
- Every integration needs a migration and credential-rotation path.

### Standard operating workflow

Reproduce in a sandbox, capture correlation identifiers, verify identity and scopes, inspect request or delivery logs, correct the smallest failing layer, and reconcile side effects.

**Required evidence.** Retain app ID, integration owner, scopes, API version, correlation or delivery ID, timestamps, redacted diagnostics, and deployment version—never secrets.

**Diagnostic sequence.** Check authentication, authorization, version, quota, mapping, idempotency, downstream response, and data reconciliation in that order.

**Article key:** `integrations.rate_handling`

**Accountability.** Application owners implement retry behavior.

**Exceptions and failure modes.** Do not retry validation or authorization failures without correcting the request.

**Common incorrect interpretation.** Rotating access tokens is the recommended way to bypass rate limits.

**Support evidence.** Apply the integrations evidence standard above and record the article key `integrations.rate_handling` with the resulting decision.

## Permissions

**Scope.** Ownership, roles, permission grants, record and field access, denial policy, sharing, identity, sessions, delegated administration, approvals, audit, and access reviews.

### Governing principles

- Least privilege and separation of duties govern every grant.
- Effective access must be diagnosed from all contributing grants and constraints.
- Presentation changes are not security controls.
- Emergency and delegated authority are scoped, expiring, and auditable.

### Standard operating workflow

Verify the subject and resource, preview effective access, identify every grant and constraint, change the owning policy rather than layering workarounds, then retest and record approval.

**Required evidence.** Retain requester, approver, reason, scope, effective and expiry times, before/after access, and the resulting audit-event ID.

**Diagnostic sequence.** Inspect base role, sets, groups, ownership, hierarchy, sharing, field rules, session state, and explicit denials before changing access.

**Exceptions and failure modes.** A broader grant cannot override an applicable explicit denial.

## Reporting

**Scope.** Report models, building and sharing, viewer security, filters, formulas, joins, dashboards, refresh, subscriptions, schedules, recipients, exports, history, currency, timezone, and recovery.

### Governing principles

- Reports never create permission to underlying data.
- Grain, running identity, freshness, and conversion context are part of a result's meaning.
- Exports inherit classification and retention obligations.
- Snapshots preserve rendered evidence, not restorable CRM data.

### Standard operating workflow

Reproduce under the same running identity, confirm source freshness, inspect report type and grain, expand filters, validate formulas and conversions, then compare a small record sample.

**Required evidence.** Retain report and dashboard IDs, definition version, running identity, refresh time, filter context, timezone, currency basis, and representative record IDs.

**Diagnostic sequence.** Do not make totals match by broadening access; isolate security, freshness, model, filter, grouping, formula, and conversion effects separately.

**Exceptions and failure modes.** Changing report type can change row meaning and is not a cosmetic operation.

## Onboarding

**Scope.** Workspace creation, trials, implementation governance, discovery, identity, domain verification, invitations, migration preparation and execution, training, readiness, go-live, and rollback.

### Governing principles

- The customer owns source-data quality and business acceptance.
- Identity and recovery are designed before broad user activation.
- Migration is rehearsed and reconciled by stable identifiers.
- Go-live is an explicit risk decision with tested rollback.

### Standard operating workflow

Define owners and acceptance criteria, configure and test in non-production, rehearse with representative data, reconcile, train users, run readiness review, authorize cutover, and monitor early-life support.

**Required evidence.** Retain requirements sign-off, mappings, source checksums, validation manifest, reconciliation totals, test evidence, readiness decisions, cutover log, and rollback authority.

**Diagnostic sequence.** Pause progression when identity recovery, security, data reconciliation, or rollback evidence is incomplete; do not hide failures with broad bypasses.

## Escalation and answer-quality standard

Escalate billing authority or accounting-document disputes to Billing Support; compromised credentials or unexplained authorization to Security Support; reproducible API or data-processing failures to Technical Support; reporting discrepancies that survive identity and definition checks to Analytics Support; and blocked cutover or reconciliation failures to Onboarding Support.

A gold-quality support answer is self-contained, uses the current-quarter article, does not expose benchmark metadata, and states the rule plus the operational consequence asked by the customer. When the customer repeats a common misconception, correct it explicitly without blaming the customer. When evidence is insufficient, request the missing workspace state rather than fabricating a result.
